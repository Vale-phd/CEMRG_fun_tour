Run your hand along these walls, grey Kentish ragstone worn smooth by the centuries, and you are touching the edge of a city nearly two thousand years old.

The Romans built the first circuit here, around the end of the third century, to defend the town they called Durovernum. What you see today was largely rebuilt in the Middle Ages, but it follows the very same line the Roman engineers laid down, and in places the medieval masons simply built straight up on the old Roman foundations.

Walk a little further and you will come to the Dane John, a great green mound beside the walls. It looks like a castle motte, but it is far older: a Roman burial mound, landscaped into a garden with a winding path in the 1790s. Climb to the top and the whole city spreads out before you.

Once these ramparts were lined with watchmen and bristled with towers, guarding the road in from the coast. Canterbury still keeps about half of its ancient wall, one of the most complete circuits in the country, and you can walk much of it, treading the boundary that has marked this city since the legions marched away.
