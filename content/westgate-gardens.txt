Westgate Gardens unfold along the Great Stour beneath the old city wall, and they hold one truly remarkable resident.

Find the enormous plane tree near the river. This Oriental plane is around two hundred years old, and its trunk has swelled to some twenty-five feet around, so vast that, over the long decades, it has slowly swallowed the circular iron bench that once stood about its base. Look closely and you can still see the metal emerging from the living wood, the tree quietly eating its own seat.

The gardens themselves were a gift. Much of this ground was once under the river, where the Roman wall met the water. The flint tower house beside you was an old bastion of that city wall, later turned into a home, and in 1936 its owners, Stephen and Catherine Williamson, gave the house and gardens to the people of Canterbury for ever. Catherine would go on to become the city's first woman mayor.

So wander the lawns, follow the river, and let the colour and the birdsong do their gentle work. Just don't forget to greet that extraordinary tree. It was here long before you, and it will very likely outlast us all.
