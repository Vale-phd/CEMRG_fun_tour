Pause here a moment, in the Buttermarket, before the great gate of the cathedral.

This is the Canterbury War Memorial, unveiled in 1921, in the raw aftermath of the First World War. Read the names carved upon it, the sons and daughters of this city, clerks and farmhands, shopkeepers and schoolboys, who left these streets and never came home. Each name was a whole life, a family, a future laid down so that others might walk here in peace.

Now lift your eyes to the magnificent gateway behind it. This is Christ Church Gate, built around 1517, the grand entrance to the cathedral precincts, encrusted with carved heraldry and angels. High in its centre stands a gilded figure of Christ, with arms outstretched. The original was torn down and destroyed by Puritan soldiers in 1642, during the Civil War, and for three and a half centuries the niche stood empty, until at last a new bronze figure was raised in 1990, and Christ returned to watch over the square once more.

War and peace, destruction and renewal, stand together in this one small space. Pause. Read a name. Look up at the gate. And then walk gently on.
