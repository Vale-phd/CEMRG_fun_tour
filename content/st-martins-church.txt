A little further east stands St Martin's Church, modest in size but extraordinary in age. It is the oldest church in the English-speaking world still in regular use, and it was already old when Augustine arrived.

Its story begins before the year 597. When the Frankish princess Bertha came to Kent to marry its pagan king, Aethelberht, she brought her Christian faith with her, and this small church, built partly from re-used Roman brick, was given to her as her private chapel. Here she worshipped, day after day, in a kingdom that had not yet been converted.

So when Augustine and his monks arrived from Rome in 597, they found a church already waiting for them. They made St Martin's their first base, preaching and baptising here before the cathedral or the abbey existed. In a very real sense, the mission that would bring Christianity to all of England began within these walls.

Look closely at the stonework and you can still see the thin red Roman bricks worked into the fabric, alongside Saxon and later medieval additions, a building that has grown and been repaired across some fifteen hundred years.

St Martin's is still a living parish church, holding services as it has done, almost without interruption, since before England itself existed. With the cathedral and St Augustine's Abbey, it completes Canterbury's World Heritage Site, and it is, quietly, where the whole story started.
