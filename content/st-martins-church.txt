Welcome to Saint Martin's Church, small, weathered, and quietly astonishing, for this is the oldest church in the English-speaking world still in regular use.

Look closely at the walls, especially around the chancel, and you will see thin red Roman bricks and tiles laid in long courses among the later stone. This building began life as a Romano-British structure, and by the time the Venerable Bede came to write his history, it was already ancient.

Its great moment came before the year 597. When the Frankish princess Bertha came across the Channel to marry Ethelbert, the pagan king of Kent, she brought her Christian faith and her own chaplain with her, and her husband gave her this little church to pray in. She named it for Saint Martin of Tours, the patron of her homeland. So when Augustine and his monks arrived from Rome in 597 to convert the English, they found a Christian queen, and a working church, already waiting for them. Augustine made Saint Martin's his first base, preaching and baptising here before the cathedral or the abbey existed.

In a very real sense, the Christianity of England began within these humble walls. Step inside, and you stand where it all started, fifteen centuries of unbroken prayer, in a church older than England itself.
