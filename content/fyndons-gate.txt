You are standing before Fyndon's Gate, the Great Gate of Saint Augustine's Abbey, and one of the most magnificent medieval gatehouses in England.

Look up at the chamber above the archway, with its tall windows. That was the abbey's grand state bedroom, and it has hosted a king. The gate was rebuilt between 1301 and 1309 by Abbot Thomas Fyndon, whose name it still carries, faced with flint and pale stone and flanked by soaring turrets to impress every pilgrim, bishop, and monarch who passed beneath.

And monarchs did. In 1625, the newly married Charles the First brought his young French bride, Henrietta Maria, to lodge in that very chamber above your head, their first nights together as king and queen spent over this gateway. The green in front of you has been called Lady Wootton's Green ever since the days of Charles, after a noblewoman who lived among the abbey ruins.

When Henry the Eighth dissolved the abbey, this gate was simply too splendid to lose, and so it survived, though German bombs in the Second World War damaged it so badly that it had to be carefully rebuilt. Today it is the gateway to the King's School, and its pupils pass beneath its seven-hundred-year-old arch every single day.
