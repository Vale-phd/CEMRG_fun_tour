Look up. Yes, up here. It's me, the tower.

I'm all that's left, if I'm honest. There was a whole church once, Saint George's, and a fine one too. In fifteen sixty-four a baby was christened at my font, a tanner's son named Christopher Marlowe, who grew up to shake the English stage. Then, one night in nineteen forty-two, the bombs came. The city burned. My church fell around me.

But I, stubborn old thing, refused to topple. So here I stand, alone among the new shops, keeping watch, and remembering. Give us a wave on your way past. It gets lonely up here.
