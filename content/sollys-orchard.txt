Solly's Orchard is one of Canterbury's quiet secrets, a small green corner tucked beside the Great Stour, just off the busy riverside path.

There are no grand ruins here and no famous graves. The pleasure of this place is simpler than that. Find the water's edge and watch the Stour slip past. This is the same river that once powered the city's mills, carried its trade, and filled the moat around its walls, here slowed to a gentle, reed-fringed drift.

Spots like this matter more than they seem. A walled medieval city packed its houses tight, and these green breathing-spaces by the water were where ordinary people came to mend their nets, graze a few animals, or simply escape the crush of the streets. An orchard would once have meant apples and cherries, for Kent is, after all, the Garden of England, and a welcome harvest right beside the town.

So linger a moment, and let the rest of the tour wait. A bench, the murmur of the water, perhaps a passing swan. Sometimes the most ordinary corner of an ancient city is exactly the place you should stop, and simply be.
