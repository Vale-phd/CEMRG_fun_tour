Greyfriars Chapel is unlike any other building in Canterbury, for it is built right across the river, the Great Stour flowing clean beneath its stone arches.

Walk around and look underneath, and you will see the water passing straight through the base of the building. This is the oldest Franciscan building in England. The Grey Friars, the followers of Saint Francis of Assisi, landed at Dover in 1224, while Francis himself was still alive, and a few of them walked on to Canterbury to found the very first Franciscan house in the country. They owned nothing at all. They begged their bread, and tended the poor and the sick.

The little chapel you see, raised over the Stour around 1267, is the last surviving fragment of what was once a large friary, spread across the water-meadows between two arms of the river. When the monasteries fell in the sixteenth century, almost all of it vanished, but this small building, perhaps a guest house or the warden's lodging, somehow survived.

Stand on the bridge a moment and listen to the water beneath the stones. After eight hundred years, this is still a place built for stillness, a fragment of Franciscan simplicity in the middle of a busy city.
